A neural network is a computer system loosely inspired by the human brain that learns to recognize patterns from data rather than following strict, pre-programmed rules.

**Key Ideas**

- **Neurons:** These are tiny decision-makers that receive numerical information and multiply it by a weight to decide how important that piece of data is. If the combined signal is strong enough, the neuron fires and passes the information forward.

- **Layers:** Neurons are organized into an input layer that takes in raw data, hidden layers that spot and combine patterns, and an output layer that delivers the final guess. For example, hidden layers might spot basic edges in a photo and combine them to recognize a face.

- **Training:** At first, the network makes random guesses, measures how far off it was from the right answer, and works backward to adjust its importance weights. After practicing on thousands of examples, these adjustments lock in so it can accurately recognize brand-new things.

A neural network is a digital brain that learns to spot patterns and make smart guesses by practicing on examples and fixing its own mistakes.
