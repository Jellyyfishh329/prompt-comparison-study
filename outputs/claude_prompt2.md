Think of a neural network as a team of tiny decision-makers working together to learn a pattern, like recognizing a cat in a photo.

**The structure:** It’s built in layers. The first layer receives the raw input, like the pixels of an image. Hidden layers in the middle process it step by step. The final layer gives the answer: “cat” or “not cat.”

**The neurons:** Each neuron takes in numbers, multiplies each by a “weight” (how important that input is), adds them up, and passes the result along if it’s strong enough. Early layers spot simple things like edges, and later layers combine them into ears, whiskers, and faces.

**The learning:** At first, the weights are random, so the guesses are terrible. The network makes a guess, compares it to the correct answer, and measures how wrong it was. Then it nudges every weight slightly in the direction that would have made the guess better. This is called backpropagation.

**The practice:** Repeat this thousands of times with thousands of examples, and the weights settle into values that make accurate predictions, even on pictures it has never seen.

It’s like a student doing practice problems: guess, check the answer, adjust, repeat.

**In short:** A neural network learns by guessing, measuring its mistakes, and adjusting its weights until its guesses become accurate.
