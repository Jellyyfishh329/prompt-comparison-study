A neural network is a computer system that learns patterns from examples, loosely inspired by how brain cells connect.

**Key Ideas**

- **Neurons:** Each neuron is a tiny calculator that takes in numbers, does a simple calculation, and passes a result forward. On its own it’s not very smart, but thousands working together can do impressive things.

- **Layers:** Neurons are arranged in layers: an input layer that receives data (like the pixels of a photo), hidden layers that process it, and an output layer that gives the answer (like “cat” or “dog”).

- **Weights:** Every connection between neurons has a weight, which is a number showing how important that signal is. Think of it as a volume knob: turning it up makes a signal matter more.

- **Training:** The network starts with random weights, so its first guesses are poor. We show it many examples with the correct answers, and it compares its guess to the truth to measure its error.

- **Learning:** After each mistake, the network adjusts its weights slightly to do better next time, a method called backpropagation. Repeating this thousands of times makes the guesses steadily more accurate.

**Summary:** A neural network learns by guessing, checking its mistakes, and tweaking its connections until it gets good at the task.
