Think of a neural network like a team of students passing notes to solve a puzzle, loosely inspired by how the human brain works. Instead of following strict, pre-programmed rules, it learns to recognize patterns through trial and error.

The network is organized into three main stages:

- **Input Layer:** Takes in raw information, like the pixels of a photo.

- **Hidden Layers:** The "thinking" middle layers where the team spots simple patterns—like edges—and combines them into complex shapes, like a face.

- **Output Layer:** Delivers the final guess, like saying, "That's a cat!"

To make decisions, tiny decision-makers called **neurons** weigh how important each piece of information is. At first, the network makes wild, random guesses. Every time it gets an answer wrong, it measures how far off it was and works backward to adjust its internal "importance" settings. After practicing on thousands of examples, those adjustments click into place, allowing it to accurately recognize brand-new things it has never seen before.

A neural network is a digital brain that learns to spot patterns and make smart guesses by practicing on examples and fixing its own mistakes.
